Codebase & AI Reliability Audit

01 · Fixed scope · one repo or workflow

Find the real failure modes before they reach customers.

I read your repository cold and trace the paths that carry money, data and trust: background jobs, webhooks, payments, auth and tenant boundaries, LLM and MCP tool calls, error tracking and tests.

What you get

  • Findings backed by exact file paths, line numbers and reproduction steps.
  • Clear separation between confirmed issues, open questions and acceptable trade-offs.
  • A prioritized fix plan, plus a walkthrough call with you or your team.
  • Optional: a key-flow accessibility check on sign-up, checkout or a core form—especially useful on AI-generated UIs.

The report is yours to keep. Your team can act on it directly, or carry the findings straight into a hardening sprint.

AI & MCP Architecture Review

02 · Advisory · before you build or refactor

Get workflow boundaries right before code hardens around them.

For teams adding LLM features, agents, MCP servers or clients, or structured outputs to a real product—and wanting a seasoned second pair of eyes before committing to an architecture.

What we cover

  • Task state, atomic claims, retries, timeouts and cancellation across background jobs and external providers.
  • Tool permission tiers, server-side authorization and human-in-the-loop approval points.
  • Evaluation strategy, regression gates, tracing and token/cost visibility.
  • A written architecture note and review session your team can build from.

Production Hardening Sprint

03 · Fixed scope · fixes in your repository

Turn a shaky or AI-built app into something you can ship and maintain.

Follows an audit, or starts directly on an agreed set of broken or unfinished flows. The goal is to keep what works and fix what doesn't—not push a rewrite.

A sprint usually includes

  • Agreed fixes made on branches in your repository, ready for review.
  • A minimal regression test net around sign-up, payments/webhooks and the core user or AI workflow.
  • Real error tracking wired up, silent catches removed, and external calls guarded with timeouts and idempotency.
  • Optional: accessibility fixes on sign-up, checkout or key forms (also available standalone).
  • A plain-English handover: what changed, how to release and roll back, and what's still worth watching.

Once a Rails app is stable, it can roll straight into Rails maintenance without repeating discovery.

Questions

Things people usually ask.

What tech stacks do you work with?

Ruby on Rails and the Ruby AI ecosystem (RubyLLM, ActiveAgent, MCP Ruby SDK) are where I am deepest, from audit all the way to hands-on code fixes. For general web, AI and MCP apps in other stacks, I audit architecture, protocols, reliability, permissions and user flows directly—and tell you honestly up front if hands-on implementation is better done by your team or a specialist in that stack.

Do you help with apps built using Cursor, Claude Code, Lovable or Bolt?

Yes. How the code was written doesn't matter much to me; what matters is how the app behaves with real users and how hard it is to maintain. A reliability audit and a production hardening sprint are designed to stabilize what you already built without pushing an unnecessary rewrite.

Will you tell me to rewrite my app?

Not unless there's a real structural blocker. A working app with real users already holds product decisions you paid to discover. The default goal is to keep what works, fix the risky paths and wrap them in tests. If a rewrite genuinely makes more sense, I'll explain why with evidence.

Can an audit include accessibility for sign-up, checkout or forms?

Yes. AI-generated front ends and fast-moving product flows often ship broken keyboard focus, unlabeled inputs or silent dialogs. Key-flow accessibility checks and fixes can be included inside an audit or hardening sprint, or booked on their own.

Is this a formal security audit or compliance certification?

No. This is practical production engineering review—closing concurrency races, missing server-side auth or tenant checks, unsafe tool boundaries and brittle external calls. Regulated apps (HIPAA, PCI, SOC 2) still need dedicated security testing and formal compliance review.

What if I want ongoing maintenance after hardening?

For Ruby on Rails apps, you can roll straight from a hardening sprint into monthly Rails maintenance without repeating discovery. You can also take the audit report or finished handover and run it in-house—both are fine.

What access do you need, and do you use AI tools on my code?

You keep full ownership of your code and hosting. I only ask for the repository or staging access the work needs, and we agree up front whether AI tools may see your code or data. Please don't send passwords, production data or API keys in your first email.
