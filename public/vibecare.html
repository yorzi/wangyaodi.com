Two services

Fix it, then keep it working.

AI App Rescue

For a broken or unfinished app

Get the app working, and know exactly what changed.

I take a short look first, and we agree on what "fixed" means before I start.

This can include

  • Finding out why things break, and fixing the problems we agreed on.
  • Repairing failing jobs, webhooks, payments, email or sign-in.
  • Adding tests around what I change, so it stays fixed.
  • A plain-English handover: what changed and what's still risky.

New features, redesigns and rewrites are separate projects. A rescue is about making what you already have work.

AI App Care

For a working app · monthly

Ongoing maintenance for the app you already have.

Each month I set aside a fixed number of hours for your app, so problems are handled by someone who already knows the code.

This can include

  • Bug fixes for the issues you report, in the order we agree.
  • Updates to packages and frameworks, tested before they go live.
  • Monitoring: I review your error tracker and logs regularly, then flag and fix what I find.
  • Upkeep: small improvements and general housekeeping.
  • A short note each month on what I did, the hours it took and what's next.

How monthly care works

You own the code. I look after it.

This is hands-on maintenance, not software you rent. The code and the hosting stay yours, and I work inside them.

Hours: a set number of hours each month, reserved for your app. Investigating, fixing, testing, updating, reviewing errors and writing the monthly note all come out of them. We agree priorities first, and I ask before going over.

Replies: within one business day on our agreed working days. That means a first look and a next step, not always a finished fix.

Monitoring: I check your existing error tracking while I'm working on your app. Nobody is watching it overnight, and there's no uptime guarantee.

No long contract: care runs month to month. The details are agreed in writing before we start.

  1. Tell me about your app. What it does, who uses it, what you built it with and what's wrong. A short email is fine.
  2. I take a quick look and give you a straight answer. If I can help, you get a clear plan. If I can't, I'll say so.
  3. Rescue it, if it needs it. I fix what we agreed and walk you through the changes.
  4. Keep it healthy. Carry on with monthly care, or stop there. Both are fine.

Questions

Things people usually ask.

What kinds of apps do you take on?

Web apps that real people use. Rails is what I know best. If yours is built on something else, tell me what it is. I'll be honest about whether I'm the right person before we go any further.

Do I need to be technical?

No. I explain what I found and what I changed in plain English. You decide what gets done and when it goes live.

Will you rewrite my app?

Not unless there's a good reason. The aim is to keep what works and fix what doesn't. If a rewrite really is the better option, I'll explain why and we'll plan it as a separate project.

How do we get started?

Email me what the app does, what you built it with and what's going wrong. I'll take a quick look and tell you whether a rescue or monthly care is the right place to start. Everything is agreed in writing before any work starts.

What access do you need, and who owns the code?

The code is yours, and you keep control of the repository and hosting. I work in your repository, every change can be reviewed, and I only ask for the access the work needs. Please don't email passwords, production data or API keys.

Do you use AI tools on my code?

We agree on that before I start, including whether AI tools can see your code or data. If you'd rather they didn't, just say so.
