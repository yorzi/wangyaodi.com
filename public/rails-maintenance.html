How to start

Start with one problem.

Focused Assessment

One-time · up to four hours

A close look at one problem before you commit to anything bigger.

We pick one app and one main concern: a job that keeps failing, a fragile integration, an upgrade that's stuck, or an AI workflow you don't trust.

What you get

  • What I found, backed by the code, logs or behavior I could check.
  • Which parts are confirmed and which are still open questions.
  • What I'd do next, in order of priority.
  • A proposed fix, or a plan for digging further, with an estimate.

It covers up to four hours of investigation, conversation and write-up. You'll usually have it within five business days of the start date, once I have the access and information I need.

It's a focused look, not a full audit, and four hours won't always be enough to find the root cause. The findings are yours to keep either way, even if you take them to another engineer.

Stabilization Sprint

Fixed scope · planned after the assessment

Fix the agreed problem and leave the app easier to look after.

After the assessment I send a proposal with the scope, how we'll know it's done and the timeline. Nothing starts until you've agreed to it.

A sprint usually includes

  • The agreed changes, made in your repository.
  • Tests or other checks that show the fix works.
  • A short summary of what changed and how I checked it.
  • Notes on how to release it, and how to roll it back.
  • A handover, plus a list of anything still worth watching.

Typical examples: webhooks being processed twice, a background workflow that keeps failing, or an AI provider changing its response format and breaking a feature.

What the assessment found carries straight into the sprint, so nothing gets investigated twice.

How it works

From first email to first fix.

  1. Email me. Tell me what the app does and what needs attention. A few lines is plenty. If it looks like a fit, we can have a free 20-minute call.
  2. Agree on the assessment. I confirm the access I need, when I can start and what I'll look at.
  3. Decide what's next. Go ahead with a fix, sign up for monthly care, handle it in-house, or stop there.
  4. Ship carefully. We agree who approves, deploys and checks each change in production.

Questions

Things people usually ask.

How do we get started?

Email me a few lines about your app and what needs attention. I'll reply with how I'd approach it and where I'd start. Scope, timing and the rest of the details are agreed in writing before any work starts.

Do you work on older Rails apps?

Yes, after a look. I start by checking the Ruby and Rails versions, the dependencies, the tests and how the app is deployed. Big version upgrades are planned as their own project. They won't fit into a small monthly plan.

Can you take over an app built with AI?

Yes, if it's mainly a Rails app and the assessment shows a sensible way forward. I judge code by how it behaves and how easy it is to maintain, whoever or whatever wrote it.

Can I keep making changes myself?

Of course. We'll agree on how changes get made and released. If changes made outside that process cause problems, sorting them out comes out of the monthly hours.

What if something you shipped breaks?

For 14 days after you accept a delivery, I'll fix any bug caused by that work that we can reproduce. New requests, unrelated bugs, and problems from later changes by other people or third parties are handled as new work.

How do you handle access and AI tools?

We agree on access and data handling before I start, including whether AI tools can see your code or data. I only ask for the access the work needs. Please don't put passwords, production data or API keys in your first email.

When are you available?

I'm in UTC+8 and work mostly asynchronously, with some overlap with US and European hours that we agree up front. Working days, overlap hours, holidays and time off are all in the proposal before you commit. Start dates depend on availability.

Tell me what's going on with your app.

What does it do, who relies on it, and what's taking up most of your time right now? I'll tell you honestly whether I can help and where I'd start.
